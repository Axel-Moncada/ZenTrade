## 8. Position sizing for prop firm evaluations
Blog: `/blog/position-sizing-prop-firm-evaluation`

### Thread de X
1. Most evaluations don't fail on time. They fail on size. Here's how to size from your drawdown, not your profit target:
2. Contracts = (buffer × risk %) ÷ (stop in points × $ per point). Round down.
3. $2,000 buffer, 12.5% risk = $250. One NQ with a 20-pt stop risks $400. That's 20% of your account on one trade.
4. Same trade on MNQ ($2/pt): 6 contracts = $240. Same idea, survivable size.
5. Buffer drops to $1,200? Risk drops to $150, size to 3 MNQ. Most traders skip this step. [link]

**Hook alternativo:** "I need $3,000, so I'll trade 2 NQ" is how most $50K evaluations end.

### Caption LinkedIn/Instagram
Size from the buffer, not the target. One formula, a contract value table (NQ, MNQ, ES, MES, GC, MGC per CME specs) and worked examples on a $50K account. Educational, not financial advice. #positionsizing #mnq #futurestrading #propfirm #zentrade

## 9. How to stop overtrading futures
Blog: `/blog/how-to-stop-overtrading-futures`

### Thread de X
1. Overtrading isn't a willpower problem. It's a missing rule. 3 rules that fix it:
2. Trade cap from YOUR data: count trades on your best days. That's the cap.
3. 15-minute pause after every loss. It removes the trade when your judgment is worst.
4. Three exits for the session: cap reached, daily limit/target hit, end time. Whichever first.
5. Then tag planned vs impulsive for 2 weeks and compare P&L. The number convinces you. [link]

**Hook alternativo:** Sort your trades by trade number in the day. Trade #5 onward is usually where the profit goes.

### Caption LinkedIn/Instagram
The test that ends overtrading arguments: sort your trades by their position in the day and add up P&L for each. For many traders, the first 2-3 trades make the money and everything after gives it back. Full guide with a trade cap table and a pause rule. #overtrading #tradingpsychology #futurestrading #propfirm #zentrade
