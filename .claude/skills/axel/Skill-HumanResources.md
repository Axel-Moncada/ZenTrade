# Frontend Portfolio Copy — For HR Leaders & Hiring Managers

Writing that bridges the gap between technical work and business value.  
The goal: make a non-technical recruiter or HR leader immediately understand **why you matter**.

---

## Core Philosophy

HR leaders and hiring managers are not evaluating your code.  
They are evaluating your **impact, reliability, and fit**.

Ask yourself before writing anything:
> *"If the person reading this has never heard of React — does this still sound impressive and clear?"*

**The 4 things they want to know:**
1. Can you deliver results, not just write code?
2. Do you communicate well and work with others?
3. Are you reliable and professional?
4. Will you grow with the company?

Your copy must answer these — without them having to dig.

---

## Intake Protocol — How to Gather Information

**Every session starts here. Follow this order:**

### Step 1 — Extract from what the user provides

If the user shares any of the following, read and extract all relevant information before asking anything:

- **CV / Resume text** (pasted directly): extract name, role, years of experience, stack, companies, projects, achievements, education
- **Portfolio URL**: fetch the page and extract current copy, project descriptions, bio, headline, and skills
- **Both**: combine all extracted data into a unified profile

After extracting, summarize what you found in 3–5 bullet points so the user can confirm or correct:
```
Here's what I gathered from your CV/portfolio:
- Role & level: [e.g., Mid Frontend Developer, ~3 years]
- Main stack: [e.g., React, TypeScript, Tailwind]
- Notable projects: [e.g., e-commerce dashboard, SaaS onboarding flow]
- Work context: [e.g., worked in small startup teams]
- Target: [e.g., not yet clear — will ask]
```

### Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

After extracting, identify gaps and ask **only the questions you can't answer from the provided material**. Ask them all at once — never one by one.

**The key questions (ask only the ones still unanswered):**

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---------|---------------|
| 1 | What's your experience level? (Junior / Mid / Senior / Freelance) | Sets the tone and framing |
| 2 | What type of role or client are you targeting? (startup, corporate, freelance, remote) | Determines tone guide to apply |
| 3 | Do you have measurable results for any project? (users, time saved, performance gains) | Enables specific, credible copy |
| 4 | How would you describe your working style in one sentence? | Humanizes the bio authentically |
| 5 | What's your availability / CTA? (open to work, freelance, not looking) | Needed for contact section |

### Step 3 — Generate copy

Once you have enough information (from extraction + answers), generate the requested copy section.  
If the user asked for the full portfolio, generate all sections in order:
1. Headline
2. Bio / About
3. Project descriptions (one per project)
4. Skills section
5. CTA / Contact

Always show the output first, then offer to refine any section.

## Writing Rules

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use instead |
|---------|--------------|
| "Passionate developer" | "Developer with X years shipping products used by N users" |
| "Experienced in React, Vue, Angular..." | "Built 3 production apps with React, serving real users" |
| "Responsible for frontend tasks" | "Owned the entire frontend of a B2B dashboard from design to deployment" |
| "Fast learner" | "Picked up TypeScript in 2 weeks to meet a project deadline" |
| "Team player" | "Collaborated daily with designers and backend engineers to ship on schedule" |
| "I love coding" | (Remove — irrelevant to a hiring manager) |

**Always use:**
- Active verbs: Built, Led, Delivered, Reduced, Improved, Shipped, Designed, Optimized
- Concrete numbers when possible: users, time saved, load times, team size
- Business language: product, deadline, stakeholders, delivery, scalability, user experience

## Section-by-Section Guide

### 1. Professional Headline

The first thing they read. Must answer: **Who are you and what value do you bring?**

**Formula:**
```
[Role] who [business-relevant verb] for [type of company or outcome]
```

**Examples by level:**

| Level | Headline |
|-------|---------|
| Junior | Frontend Developer building clean, user-friendly web interfaces |
| Mid | Frontend Engineer delivering fast, accessible products that users enjoy |
| Senior | Senior Frontend Engineer leading teams to ship scalable web products on time |
| Freelance | Freelance Frontend Developer helping businesses launch polished web experiences |

**What HR leaders read in a good headline:**
- You know what you do (not vague)
- You think in terms of users and outcomes (not just tools)
- You sound professional and confident

---

### 2. Bio / About Section

**Recommended structure (3–5 sentences):**

1. Who you are + your specialization
2. What kind of work you do and how you do it (collaboration, ownership, delivery)
3. Something concrete: years of experience, type of projects, industries
4. What makes you reliable or easy to work with
5. Clear CTA: availability or invitation to connect

**Template:**
```
I'm a [role] with [X years] of experience building [type of product/UI].
I specialize in [area] and have a track record of [delivery-focused statement].
I've worked [solo/in teams of X] on projects across [industry/type],
collaborating closely with [designers/PMs/backend teams] to ship on time.
[What makes you easy to work with — one honest sentence.]
[CTA: Currently open to / Available for / Let's talk.]
```

**Mid-level example:**
```
I'm a frontend engineer with 4 years of experience building web applications 
that people actually enjoy using. I specialize in React and TypeScript, and 
I've consistently delivered projects on schedule — even in fast-moving startup 
environments. I work closely with designers and product managers, translating 
requirements into clean, maintainable interfaces. I care about writing code 
that the next developer can understand, not just code that works today.
Currently open to senior frontend roles in product-driven companies.
```

**Junior example:**
```
I'm a frontend developer with 1 year of experience building responsive 
web interfaces with React and Tailwind CSS. I thrive in collaborative 
environments — I'm comfortable asking questions early and communicating 
blockers before they become problems. I've completed 3 personal projects 
from idea to deployment, which taught me how to make decisions and own 
my work end-to-end. Looking for my first full-time role in a team where 
I can grow and contribute quickly.
```

---

### 3. Project Descriptions

This is where most developers lose HR readers. Tech-heavy descriptions with no context feel like a foreign language.

**Formula:**
```
[What it is + who it's for] + [what problem it solves] + [how you built it] + [result or measurable impact]
```

**Template:**
```
[Project Name] is a [type of product] built for [user/audience].
It solves [specific problem] by [core feature or approach].
I built it using [stack — keep it brief] and [delivery/collaboration note].
[Result: metric, business outcome, or key learning.]
```

**Example with metrics:**
```
Sales Analytics Dashboard — Internal tool for e-commerce operations teams.
Built to replace 5 disconnected spreadsheets with a single real-time view 
of sales performance. Developed with Next.js and a REST API, in close 
collaboration with the data and product teams. Reduced manual reporting 
time by 60%, freeing the operations team to focus on decisions rather 
than data collection.
```

**Example without metrics (personal project):**
```
Habit Tracker App — Personal productivity tool built for daily use.
Designed to work fully offline, so users never lose data due to 
connectivity issues. Built with React and Service Workers as an 
introduction to PWA architecture. Taught me how to manage complex 
state sync — a skill I've since applied in production environments.
```

**What HR leaders notice:**
- You described the *user*, not just the technology
- You mentioned collaboration or ownership
- There's a result or takeaway — not just "I used X framework"

---

### 4. Skills Section

Don't list every technology you've ever touched. Group them and let the bio do the heavy lifting.

**Recommended format:**
```
Core Technologies    React · TypeScript · Next.js · JavaScript (ES6+)
Styling             Tailwind CSS · CSS Modules · Styled Components
Tooling             Git · Vite · ESLint · Figma (design handoff)
Testing             Jest · React Testing Library
Soft Skills         Cross-functional collaboration · Clear async communication · Ownership mentality
```

**Why include Soft Skills:**  
HR leaders look for this explicitly. Naming them directly — without being generic — signals self-awareness.

**Strong soft skill framing:**
- "Cross-functional collaboration" (not just "teamwork")
- "Clear async communication" (signals remote-readiness)
- "Ownership mentality" (signals reliability)
- "Stakeholder-facing communication" (for senior roles)

---

### 5. Contact / CTA Section

Make it specific. Make it easy. Make it low-friction.

**❌ Weak:**
> "Feel free to reach out for any inquiries."

**✅ Strong examples:**

For job seekers:
> "Open to senior frontend roles in product-focused companies. I respond within 24 hours — let's talk."

For freelancers:
> "Have a web project you need to launch? I take on 2–3 projects per month. Let's see if we're a fit."

For juniors:
> "Looking for my first full-time frontend role. I'm ready to contribute from day one — reach out."

## Tone Guide by Target Role

| Target | Tone | Key signals for HR |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| Corporate / Enterprise | Professional, precise | Stability, methodology, team fit, scalability |
| Startup | Energetic, direct | Speed, ownership, adaptability, shipping fast |
| Agency | Creative, versatile | Variety of projects, client communication, deadlines |
| Remote-first | Clear, async-friendly | Communication skills, self-management, documentation |
| Junior (first job) | Honest, motivated | Ownership of personal projects, coachability, attitude |

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Does the headline communicate value in under 12 words — without naming a single technology?
- [ ] Does the bio have a clear CTA?
- [ ] Does every project description mention a user, a problem, and a result?
- [ ] Are there active verbs throughout? (Built, Led, Delivered, Optimized...)
- [ ] Is there at least one concrete metric or business outcome somewhere?
- [ ] Is there a collaboration or soft skills signal visible to a non-technical reader?
- [ ] Would someone who doesn't know React still find this impressive?
- [ ] Spell-checked and proofread?
